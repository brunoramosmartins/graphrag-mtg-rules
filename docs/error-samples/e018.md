# E-018 — manual error sample

Standing rule 8 deliverable, read 2026-09-13, before any figure from E-018 is
quoted outside the registry. It supersedes the coarser reading in that entry's
`Actual result`, which said "nine answered and were judged wrong" and left it
there.

**What is here.** The nine questions the ceiling judged **derivable** and where
the treatment condition answered and was scored not `correct`. Nine is the whole
category, so it is read in full rather than sampled.

**What is not here, and why.** No Comprehensive Rules text, no answer keys, and
no RulesGuru question text. `docs/` is committed and the Fan Content Policy and
the golden set's licence posture forbid all three; the ceiling worksheet lives
in gitignored `data/interim/` for the same reason. Rule *numbers*, card
*names*, the model's own prose and the judge's own rationale are ours or are
identifiers, and they are quoted. Anyone with the repo can reproduce the full
rendering:

```
python scripts/e018_inspect.py --qid rg-1591 --condition treatment --full
```

**Population.** 20 primary questions; ceiling 17 derivable; treatment correct
on 7 of those 17; **10 not correct**, of which one (`rg-3155`) declined and is
covered at the end. The nine below answered.

## The decomposition, which is the finding

*Revised 2026-09-13 after a review challenged three of the four groups. Two
challenges were upheld against the first version of this file and one landed
harder than it was put. What follows is the revision; the first version's
claims are struck where they were wrong rather than edited away.*

| group | n | what actually failed |
|---|---:|---|
| **A — the rule was cited and misapplied** | 3 | reasoning, with the governing rule quoted in the answer |
| **B — multi-step simulation** | 2 | counting loop iterations or maximising over orderings; the CR rule is not the obstacle |
| **C — reached the key's verdict, scored not `correct`** | 3 | two by the rubric working as designed, one by the judge breaking its own tie-break |
| **D — reasoned from a different rule** | 1 | reasoning, but the injected rule never entered the argument |

"The generator fails with the evidence in hand" covers **A and D — four of

## C — reached the key's verdict and was scored not `correct` (3 of 9)

> ~~These two are the measurement, not the system. Counting them as generation
> failures counts a rubric completeness criterion as a reasoning defect.~~
> **Withdrawn — the review was right and this was an overreach.** The rubric
> does not dock completeness by accident; it does it on purpose. `partial` is
> defined as *"reaches the key's verdict but is **materially incomplete**"*,
> tie-break 5 makes missing an element the key treats as part of its verdict a
> `partial`, and tie-break 3 states the intent outright: **"A judge-level
> answer is the explanation, not the yes."** Under the two-way collapse E-001
> registered, these are failures **by the measure the entry chose**. What the
> grouping changes is the **causal interpretation**, not the score — and saying
> "the measurement, not the system" collapsed those two things.

So this group is kept, renamed, and its claim narrowed to: *the verdict was
right and what was missing was the justification the rubric requires*. Whether
that is worth fixing is a product question about what a judge-level answer is
for, not evidence that retrieval or reasoning failed.

The judge sits at its weakest here regardless: **E-011a measured it agreeing
with a human on `partial` 4 of 14**, and E-011 declined to publish correctness
as validated.

- [x] **`rg-396`** — injected `707.9a`, cited nothing injected. Judge: *"The
  answer **correctly concludes** that the token will be exiled but does not
  explicitly state that the exile ability is part of the copiable values."* The
  answer says the new token carries the exile ability; the missing sentence is
  the justification, not the verdict.

- [x] **`rg-650`** — injected `614.6`, cited nothing injected. Judge: *"The
  answer **correctly identifies** the replacement effect of Enduring Renewal
  but does not fully address the outcomes related to Keranos."*

- [x] **`rg-2711`** — moved here from B. The key says **"One."**, the answer
  says **1**, and the judge returned `incorrect`. Under tie-break 3 the worst
  available label for a right verdict reached by reasoning the key contradicts
  is `partial`. **This one is not the rubric working as designed; it is the
  judge not following it**, and it is the only case in this sample where the
  instrument is simply wrong rather than strict.

**Consequence:** two of these are the registered measure doing what it was
written to do, and one is a judge error. None of the three is evidence that the
model could not reason from the evidence it had.

## The tenth: a correct refusal, and a disagreement with the ceiling

- [x] **`rg-3155`** — injected `120.6`, **cited `120.6`**, and **declined**:

  > *"Since the context does not provide Silent Artisan's toughness, I cannot
  > definitively conclude whether it dies or not.*
  > *CANNOT ANSWER. The toughness of Silent Artisan is missing from the
  > context."*

  The model reasoned from the injected rule, found a card attribute absent, and
  refused as the prompt instructs. **This is retrieval, not generation.**

  It also **contradicts the ceiling verdict** for this question, which the
  author marked `derivable: true`.

  **Reopened against the rendered context, 2026-09-13, and the model is
  right.** The key's verdict is *"Yes"* — Silent Artisan dies — and reaching it
  requires knowing its toughness, because the damage is lethal only relative to
  it. The context contains:

  ```
  [card:Expedition Envoy]
  [card:Silent Artisan]
  ```

  **Both with empty text.** Both are vanilla creatures, and `card_core` emits
  only `card_text`, so a card with no oracle text arrives as a **name and
  nothing else** — and **power and toughness are never serialized for any
  card**, vanilla or not. The toughness the verdict turns on was not in the
  prompt. The model said so and declined, which is what the prompt instructs.

  **Consequence:** the ceiling verdict for `rg-3155` should be `false`, which
  the author decides. If it flips, the derivable denominator is **16, not 17**,
  and every figure computed over it moves with it. The countersign box below
  covers this.

## A defect the sample found that is not about generation at all

Chasing `rg-3155` to the rendered context turned up a retrieval gap nothing in
this project had recorded. Measured across the whole evaluation split:

| arm | card evidence items | arriving as **a name with empty text** | questions affected |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| A — vector | 886 | **0** | 0 |
| B — graph | 84 | **7** | **6** |
| C — hybrid | 125 | 7 | 6 |

`card_core` emits `card_text` and nothing else, so a vanilla creature reaches
the model as a bare name — and **no card, vanilla or not, ever arrives with its
power and toughness**. The vector arm indexes the whole card document and never
sends a bare name, which is one concrete thing it does that the graph arm does
not.

The six affected questions in arm B: `rg-51` (Glass Golem), `rg-2711` (Brutal
Cathar), `rg-615` (Dromoka Warrior), `rg-3155` (Expedition Envoy, Silent
Artisan), `rg-4853` (Orazca Raptor), `rg-396` (Sliver Construct).

**Three of them — `rg-51`, `rg-2711`, `rg-396` — are in the nine above.** Not
claimed: that the missing card data *caused* those three failures. `rg-2711` is
a judge error and `rg-396` reached the right verdict, so at most one of the
three looks causal. What is claimed is narrower and checkable: **part of the
population read here as "the model had sufficient evidence" had a context with
card data missing from it**, and that is retrieval.

It is small, it is specific, and unlike the bridge in E-022 it is cheap to
close. It does not reopen Phase 9's retrieval objective — the governing rule
still arrives on 2 of 22 and nothing here touches that.

## What this sample changes

- The registry's *"nine answered and were judged wrong"* stands as a count and
  is **misleading as a characterisation**. Four are reasoning failures with
  evidence present, two are multi-step simulation, three reached the key's
  verdict and were scored not `correct` — two of those by the rubric as
  designed and one by the judge breaking it. Countersigned 2026-09-14.
- **Phase 9's proposed revision is narrowed.** "Where the generator fails with
  the evidence in hand" is **four cases** of a derivable denominator of **16**,
  not nine or ten of seventeen. At that size it is a lead for a registered
  entry and nothing more, which is what E-023 says.
- **The ceiling lost a question and is now 16 of 20 — 0.800 [0.584, 0.919].**
  `rg-3155` was reopened against the rendered context and the model was right:
  the toughness its verdict turns on was never in the prompt. The gate was 7
  and the reading clears it either way.
- **One judge verdict is wrong against its own frozen rubric** (`rg-2711`).
  That is the first instance this project has found by reading rather than by
  the E-011 audit, and it is one of 20 — not a rate, a case.
- **A retrieval defect surfaced that nothing was looking for**: seven card
  items across six questions arrive as a bare name, and power and toughness are
  never serialized at all. Zero occurrences in the vector arm.

Three of these five were produced by a review challenging the first version of
this file. Two of the challenges were upheld, one was upheld harder than it was
made, and the fourth item — the judge error — came out of checking one of them.
