## Verdict: nothing is adopted, and arm A stays at published defaults

Pin 7 permits tuning arm A on the 20 development questions and requires the
sweep published. It was run — 588 cells plus three follow-up probes, no LLM
call — and **it found nothing adoptable.**

The apparent winner moved every time the grid was extended:

    depth ≤ 200      11/26
    depth ≤ 1600     16/26
    depth ≤ 12800    19/26

At 12,800 candidates the arm is reading **11% of a 115,547-document corpus
per query**. The registered stopping rule, fixed before the curve existed,
covers this case: if the curve keeps rising to the largest depth swept,
nothing is adopted, and the finding is that **the objective is degenerate**.

And the one parameter that looked like a genuine tuning gain is not one.
`b = 0.4` dominated every top cell of the 588-cell sweep. Tested at
defensible depths it does nothing at all:

| depth | b=0.4 | b=0.75 | b=1.0 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 50 | 7/26 | 7/26 | 7/26 |
| 100 | 9/26 | 9/26 | 9/26 |
| 200 | 9/26 | 9/26 | 9/26 |
| 400 | 10/26 | 10/26 | 10/26 |

`b` only separates inside the degenerate regime, so it falls with it.
Likewise `k1`, `rrf_k` and `iterative`: all tie wherever the depth is
defensible. **Nothing in the classical BM25 or fusion tuning space moves
this objective.**

## Why the objective is degenerate, mechanically

Gold-rule recall counts how many gold CR numbers appear in the context
*after* the token budget trims it. A larger candidate pool feeds fusion
more documents; the budget then keeps whichever ~80 of them rank highest,
and among a bigger pool more of those happen to carry a gold rule number.
The metric therefore rewards **recall into a pool**, which reading deeper
always improves, rather than retrieval precision, which it does not measure
at all.

This is the second pathology found in the same metric by an independent
route. The first, recorded in `docs/evaluation.md`, is that it is close to
blind on `interaction_multihop`, where the answer-bearing evidence is a
Scryfall ruling carrying no CR number.

## What this means for the head-to-head

**It strengthens the baseline's credibility rather than weakening it.** The
roadmap's risk register marks a strawman vector arm as the critical threat,
and the standard objection — "you never tuned it" — no longer applies. Arm
A was tuned in good faith across 588 cells and three probes, and the sweep
declined to move it.

The claim E-001 may make is therefore precise: *the graph was compared
against a vector arm that was swept on the development split, and the sweep
found no configuration better than the published defaults on the registered
retrieval objective.*

**What it does not license.** This is not evidence that arm A is at its
optimum. It is evidence that **the registered objective cannot tell arm A's
configurations apart**, which is a different and weaker statement. A sweep
on *answer correctness* — the metric the head-to-head actually reports —
would be the informative one, and it is not free: every cell costs 15
generations plus 15 judge calls, and the judge is not yet audited above its
registered floor. That is named as future work with its cost, not folded in
here.
